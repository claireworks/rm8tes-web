Evidence · How it works

You shouldn’t have to trust us

Anyone can call their photos tamper-proof. The question a deposit scheme actually asks is harder: could this have been changed afterwards? Here is how rm8tes answers that—and how you can check the answer without taking our word for any of it.

  1. 01

    Fingerprinted as you shoot

    The moment you take a photo, your phone computes its SHA‑256 fingerprint—a 64‑character code that changes completely if so much as one pixel moves.

  2. 02

    Checked, not just claimed

    Our server recomputes that fingerprint from the file it actually received, and rejects the record if the two disagree. A fingerprint the app alone decides is worth nothing—a tampered app would simply send whatever suited it.

  3. 03

    Locked, then sealed

    Once stored, a photo cannot be edited, swapped or quietly retaken. Finish your move-in and that whole set seals shut; move-out seals the same way—and in a shared flat, only once every other flatmate has agreed it is complete. That is what stops “move-in photos” appearing months into a dispute—usually the first thing a landlord will suggest.

  4. 04

    Anchored to Bitcoin

    Roughly once an hour we fold every new record’s fingerprint into a single code and timestamp it into the Bitcoin blockchain using OpenTimestamps, a free and open-source standard. Once that block is mined the code is fixed in a public ledger nobody controls—not your landlord, and not us.

✓ Check it yourself

Your evidence pack prints every fingerprint in full, the steps linking your photo to its Bitcoin timestamp, and instructions for confirming them with free, open-source tools. Those tools talk to the public timestamp servers and to Bitcoin directly—they never contact rm8tes. If we altered one of your records, the check would fail, and you would be the one holding the proof.

Where the line is. This proves your photos existed, exactly as they are, before a particular Bitcoin block was mined, and that nobody has changed them since—us included. It cannot prove what a camera was pointed at, and a phone’s clock is set by whoever owns the phone, so your pack shows our server’s timestamp as the one that carries weight and labels the device’s own clock as context. We would rather tell you where that line sits than have a landlord’s solicitor find it for you.
